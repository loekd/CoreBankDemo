- (decision) Fault controls are the second named exception to the global single-action-in-flight lock, joining the Burst control's Cancel. Raising latency or error rate while a burst or load run is in flight is the demonstration itself, so the lock must not block it.

- (decision) Add a feedback loop from CoreBank to the DemoRunner console: the console subscribes to the transaction-events broadcast and renders pushed outcomes, closing the gap where a submitted payment sat at 202 Pending until the operator manually ran an outcome query.
- (decision) All three broadcast CloudEvents drive one loop: com.corebank.transaction.completed, com.corebank.transaction.failed, and com.corebank.account.balance.updated. Each type renders appropriately rather than collapsing into a single generic 'processed' signal.
- (decision) Confirmation surfaces in two places only: in place on the originating Operations payment row, which flips Pending to its proven outcome under the operator's finger, and in the Evidence feed, which is where a burst's outcomes accumulate because row-by-row tracking does not scale past a handful of payments.
- (decision) Rejected: a sixth Events workspace, a persistent always-visible event lane, and a full-width stage banner. The IA stays at five workspaces and the feedback loop adds no new surface.
- (decision) Fast path for this update: batch the remaining gaps, draft both spine updates with ASSUMPTION tags on unresolved interaction choices rather than blocking.
- (decision) Correlation is by TransactionId, verified in code rather than assumed: PaymentsController's 202 returns snapshot.TransactionId, and all three CloudEvents carry TransactionId. No new correlation mechanism is invented.
- (decision) The feedback loop gets its own liveness signal, an outcome-feed chip in the topology bar beside the fault chip. Without it the feature can lie on stage: a dead subscription leaves every payment at Pending while the system actually settled everything, and the operator would narrate a failure that did not happen.
- (decision) The feed chip is not teal. Teal already has exactly three roles and the Do's and Don'ts ban a fourth. Listening reuses text-secondary, Not listening reuses text-muted, and Feed lost takes state-failed because a dropped subscription is a proven failure of the console's own observation.
- (decision) Silence is never an outcome. A payment that never settles reads 'Awaiting settlement' with an elapsed readout, never Failed; only a transaction.failed event proves a rejection. This extends the existing ban on treating elapsed time as a proxy for success to its inverse.
- (decision) Burst counters split into an HTTP leg (accepted/failed) and a proven leg (settled/rejected/awaiting). The awaiting number closing live is the visual confirmation for a burst, where row-by-row tracking is useless.
- (decision) The console must never become a competing consumer on transaction-events. Observing must not alter what is observed: diverting events away from PaymentsAPI would break the very demo the loop is meant to prove. Stated as a spine invariant and handed to architecture.
- (decision) Event counts are taken from the producer, not assumed: TransactionExecutionHandler emits TransactionCompleted plus exactly two BalanceUpdated events (debit on FromAccount, credit on ToAccount) for a success, and TransactionFailed alone for a rejection. A rejected payment therefore has no balance legs, and an empty leg column under a Rejected row is correct rather than missing.
- (assumption) The transport carrying the broadcast to the console (SSE, long-poll, or a small read-only relay on an existing service) is an architecture decision. The spine requires only that it be read-only, prompt, liveness-observable, and non-competing with PaymentsAPI's consumption.
- (assumption) Transient emphasis on a settling row is a brief bold/reverse-video attribute change on that row only, never a full-screen flash or a repeating blink, and never longer than about a second. The settled state must be fully readable from a static screenshot taken later.
- (assumption) The Evidence event feed retains the most recent 500 rows and elides older ones behind an explicit count; burst counters are computed from every event received, never only from retained rows.
- (change) Key Flows renumbered: a new Flow 3 (watching two hundred payments prove themselves) joins the canonical capability sequences, pushing Journey A to Flow 4 and Journey B to Flow 5. Flow 1's recovery step now resolves pushed rather than queried, and Journey A's dedupe beat is proven by the absence of a second pair of balance legs.
- (override) Overturns the drafted outcome-feed chip. Loek rejected a seventh topology-bar chip; the topology bar stays as it was. Feed liveness is stated inline instead, parenthesised on an awaiting payment row's own elapsed readout and in the Evidence feed header.
- (decision) The inline treatment is structurally stronger than the rejected chip, not merely smaller. A chip annotates a claim that has gone stale; a row that re-states itself as Outcome unknown withdraws the claim. The console becomes incapable of displaying 'Awaiting settlement' while deaf, rather than merely warning about it elsewhere on screen.
- (decision) A dropped subscription renders state-neutral 'Outcome unknown', never red. state-failed asserts a proven failure, and a lost feed proves nothing about the payment - the money may well have moved. Grey is the accurate answer here, not a softer red.
