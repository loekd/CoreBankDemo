CoreBankDemo DemoRunner — UX Design Validation Report

docs/bmad/planning-artifacts/ux-designs/ux-CoreBankDemo-2026-09-03/ · Operations Stage-focus pass

This report describes work that is finished. Both gate lenses returned Fail. All 41 confirmed findings were accepted — none was rejected — and every one has since been applied to the spines. The verdicts below are the state the lenses found, not the state the spines are in now; each finding carries the resolution that was applied, and where the resolution differs from the fix the reviewer proposed, the resolution is what landed.

Two lenses ran as the reviewer gate: live-stage safety and recoverability and structure. Stage safety found 4 Critical, 7 High, 6 Medium and 2 Low and refused the build outright — “do not build the cancel affordance or the burst takeover from these spines as written.” Structure found 1 Critical, 7 High, 7 Medium and 7 Low, most of them ownership and restatement defects introduced by the redesign itself. The two most serious findings were both about the console asserting something it had not proven: a burst takeover that deletes every surface carrying the feed statement, and a 504 Cancelled answer that exists in the shipped system and in neither spine while three Key Flows narrate 202 Pending in its place.

Coverage is partial, and deliberately so. The rubric walker was offered and declined for this pass, so none of the eight rubric categories was assessed. The rubric review on disk (review-rubric.md) belongs to the superseded 2026-09-03 pass and predates the Stage-focus redesign, the six new components and the twelve new spacing tokens; its verdicts are stale and are not carried forward here. No accessibility lens ran this pass either. What this report covers is what the two lenses that ran actually looked at — nothing has been filled in by inference.

The prose lens ran afterwards, at polish, as the configured document standard rather than as a gate lens. It is reported separately below because it is not a gate verdict — but it was not cosmetic: it made ten substantive corrections beyond copy-editing and surfaced four wireframe-versus-spine conflicts, all four since resolved.

Rubric categories — not assessed in this pass

not assessed

The rubric walker was offered and declined for this pass, as it was declined on the two prior passes. No verdict is recorded for any of the eight rubric categories — Flow coverage, Token completeness, Component coverage, State coverage, Visual reference coverage, Bloat & overspecification, Inheritance discipline, Shape fit — because no lens assessed them. They are listed above as not assessed rather than carried over, guessed, or inferred.

A rubric review does exist on disk. It belongs to the 2026-09-03 pass and describes the pre-Stage-focus spines: the nineteen-component set, the payment form, the Evidence strip, the 22-column rail. This pass added six components, twelve spacing tokens, a fourth CloudEvent type and a redesigned Operations workspace, and deleted the Evidence strip from all five workspaces. Those verdicts no longer describe these files, so reproducing them here would be the exact dishonesty this section exists to avoid.

The structure lens did report clean results on ground adjacent to two of the categories — zero dangling {group.name} references, both palettes holding identical 16-key sets, every DESIGN component paired with an EXPERIENCE Component Patterns row, and the row and column arithmetic adding up. That is the structure lens's own integrity check on what this pass changed. It is not the rubric's token-completeness or component-coverage assessment — which walks the brief, recomputes contrast ratios and judges sufficiency, not consistency — and it is not offered as a substitute for one.

No accessibility or terminal-legibility lens ran either. The stage-safety lens raised one projector-legibility doubt (the ⊘ glyph and state-neutral for a proven outcome) but explicitly declined to raise it as a finding; it is recorded under Unsettled items.

Live-stage safety and recoverability — Fail (all findings resolved)

review-stage-safety.md

Verdict as found: Fail — “do not build the cancel affordance or the burst takeover from these spines as written.” Counts: 4 Critical, 7 High, 6 Medium, 2 Low, plus 4 items the reviewer could not settle. All 19 confirmed findings were accepted and applied.

The lens: a presenter operating alone under time pressure while talking, in front of 250 people, with no retry on visible failure. The governing principle under test is that the console must never claim something the system has not proven. Where a finding turned on what the running system actually answers, the reviewer verified it against the code rather than assuming.

In its own summary: “The Stage-focus layout is a genuine improvement and the cancel state model is more careful than most of this file's peers, but four defects would let the console assert something untrue in front of a room, and two of them sit on the exact beats the redesign was built for.” The reviewer also recorded what was already strong — the cancel state model “right where it is hardest”, the rule that Outcome unknown is never entered by a timeout (“that sentence is the single best line in this pass”), the defensibility of removing the persistent evidence strip, and colour independence holding across all five new components. It re-checked all ten findings from the superseded 2026-09-03 stage-safety pass and confirmed them still resolved.

critical

C1 — The burst takeover carries no feed statement, so the console's most-watched screen cannot say whether anyone is listening.

EXPERIENCE.md · Burst takeover / Feed status (inline); DESIGN.md · Components; wireframes S8, S9
The takeover “replaces the compose bar, the focus card and the STILL OPEN strip” — precisely the two places the feed statement is permitted to live. For the whole of a 200-payment burst the screen shows Settled 187 · still moving 13 with no statement anywhere that the console is still hearing anything. If the subscription drops mid-burst the counter freezes at 13 and keeps displaying a number that has silently changed meaning from “13 payments the console is waiting to hear about” to “13 payments nobody is listening for.” “The operator holds the room on a number that has quietly stopped being true, which is the single failure this whole capability exists to prevent.”
Resolution (applied): the takeover's captioned rule carries the feed statement at its right end, exactly as the STILL OPEN strip's rule does. On feed loss the Settled line restates the payments it can no longer account for as outcome unknown in state-neutral, rather than freezing at a stale number that reads as truth. Both burst wireframes were redrawn carrying the statement on their rule.
critical

C2 — 504 Cancelled exists in the shipped system and in neither spine; three Key Flows narrate 202 Pending for a payment the rail has already withdrawn.

EXPERIENCE.md · Voice and Tone, State Patterns, Flow 1 step 3, Flow 2 step 6, Flow 4 failure branch
Verified against PaymentsAPI/Controllers/PaymentsController.cs: on the instant rail, InstantDeliveryOutcome.Cancelled answers 504 with a frozen body whose Status is Cancelled; only Deferred answers 202. Searching both spines for 504 returned nothing. Both readings fail on stage: rendered red it dresses a cancellation as a failure; treated as unresolved it sits at ~ AWAITING SETTLEMENT with a climbing clock for a payment the rail has already withdrawn. Flow 2 step 6 is the worst case — the beat where the operator narrates cancelling a hanging payment that may have answered 504 Cancelled at submission: “He would be claiming to cause an outcome the system produced on its own.”
Resolution (applied): 504 Cancelled added to Foundation's HTTP vocabulary beside 202 Pending — the memlog records it now present in eight places in EXPERIENCE.md. Every Key Flow beat narrating 202 Pending for a budget-exhausted instant payment was corrected, and a 504 whose body does not say Cancelled is stated to remain an ordinary gateway failure. This also drove a new wireframe (a rail-initiated withdrawal at nine seconds) and, later, a seventh card state word, CANCELLED BY THE RAIL.
critical

C3 — The 409 branch prints “the bank is executing it right now” for an answer that also means terminally failed.

EXPERIENCE.md · State Patterns, Payment: Cancel refused — mid-execution (409)
TransactionCancellationOutcome.InFlight — the only outcome mapped to Conflict() — is documented in TransactionCancellationHandler.cs as Processing under a live claim, terminally Failed, or lost the claim race. Three situations, one of them terminal, and the response body carries which one it is. “The console had the answer in hand and printed a sentence that disagreed with it.” The clock keeps climbing, the card keeps offering Cancel payment, and the claim is unretractable in practice — nothing will ever arrive to correct it, because the failure already happened.
Resolution (applied): never assert execution. The card renders the status the response body actually carries — “the bank refused the cancellation and reports <Status>” — and where that status is terminal the card resolves to it rather than staying awaiting.
critical

C4 — A submission whose response never arrives has no payment state anywhere, and the strip's collapse then reads as “nothing is happening.”

EXPERIENCE.md · Foundation, Still-open strip, State Patterns; DESIGN.md · Still-open strip
Under 40% injected errors — Flow 2 step 5's exact conditions — the response never comes back. The console has nothing to correlate, nothing to put in the strip, nothing to select, and nothing for Look up outcome to look up. The strip stays collapsed, the focus card keeps showing whatever resolved before, and the screen returns to a calm, settled-looking picture “while a payment that may have reached CoreBank and moved money is outstanding and untracked.”
Resolution (applied): stronger than the fix proposed. A correction recorded in the remediation brief established that PaymentStorageHandler sets IdempotencyKey and TransactionId to the same value, so in Generated and Supplied modes the console holds the id before it sends. The focus card therefore appears at the Submit keypress carrying the request, a running clock and a live Cancel; a response that never arrives leaves the card reading that no answer has come, with the clock running. It never reads Failed, and the still-open strip counts it. In Omitted mode alone Cancel renders disabled with its reason stated — written as a further consequence of the mode already labelled “not retry-safe after an ambiguous outcome,” not as a separate limitation. This is also what makes cancelling a running instant payment reachable at all.
high

H1 — The focus card's “exactly one closing block, never two” makes a contradicted outcome unrenderable on the one object the room reads.

EXPERIENCE.md · Focus card, Contradicted outcome; DESIGN.md · focus-card tokens
An instant-rail 200 Completed followed by com.corebank.transaction.failed — which the spine itself calls “the most valuable thing the feedback loop can surface” — leaves the largest object on the screen able to render only one of the two facts. With session history gone from Operations, the card is the whole of what the room sees. “A console that silently picks a side on its most-watched surface is doing exactly what Interaction Primitives bans two sections later.”
Resolution (applied): Contradiction is the single named exception to the one-block rule and renders both claims with their sources and their times, consistent with the standing rule that the console has no tie-break and must never acquire one.
high

H2 — Cancel has no in-flight state and no answer for a reply that never arrives.

EXPERIENCE.md · Cancel payment action; Interaction Primitives
Every other command in this console has a stated “still working, not hung” affordance — all of them added by the previous stage-safety pass. Cancel is the one action that skips the modal, “so there is not even a modal as evidence it was pressed.” Under an 800–2000 ms latency band the operator presses it, nothing changes, and mid-sentence he presses it again — and a replayed cancellation publishes nothing.
Resolution (applied): the action gets a dispatched state. A cancel whose reply never arrives leaves the payment exactly as it was and says the cancellation is unconfirmed. It never implies the payment was withdrawn.
high

H3 — Whether ⊘ CANCELLED is proven by the 200 or by the broadcast is stated both ways, and one reading hangs the card forever.

EXPERIENCE.md · Cancel payment action, State Patterns, Flow 2 step 6
Three statements, two answers. Under the waiting reading, a replayed cancellation — which CoreBank documents as publishing no event — leaves the card at ~ AWAITING SETTLEMENT permanently while the console holds a 200 Cancelled in hand. “Either way the implementer picks one and the spine endorses the other.”
Resolution (applied): aligned to the shipped model. The 200 Cancelled is CoreBank's own committed statement and proves the withdrawal; the broadcast com.corebank.transaction.cancelled confirms it. The card does not hang waiting for the event, and a contradicting event produces a Contradiction.
high

H4 — The burst's Sent line prints accepted in the proven-pass green and failed in the proven-failure red.

DESIGN.md · Components, Burst control
state-healthy is “reserved for a proven pass … never for ‘probably fine’ or ‘still waiting’,” and an HTTP 202 is the console's canonical example of not proven. From the back of a 250-seat room the takeover shows two green numbers stacked in one column, accepted 200 above Settled 187 — “the burst reads as finished before the demonstration it exists for … has been made.” The red is wrong in the mirror direction: an injected 503 on the response path makes a failed send the one figure on the screen whose outcome is genuinely unknown.
Resolution (applied): the Sent line is transport, not proof, and carries no state colour at all. Only the Settled line may use the proven-pass and proven-failure tokens.
high

H5 — Failed sends are unaccounted for, and the drained takeover prints “every payment proved itself” with no stated precondition.

wireframe S9; EXPERIENCE.md · Burst control, Burst takeover
A payment whose send failed has no proven outcome, cannot be Settled or rejected, and is not stated to join still moving — it falls out of the accounting entirely. Meanwhile the drained takeover holds every payment proved itself · nothing left awaiting on screen until dismissed, “which makes it the longest-lived and loudest claim the console ever renders.” Twelve payments with unknown outcomes can sit behind that sentence. “This is the burst equivalent of C4 and it is worse, because the console states the negative out loud.”
Resolution (applied): the drained takeover states that sentence only when settled plus rejected equals sent and nothing is awaiting; otherwise it states the shortfall in the same place.
high

H6 — The transient announcement has one token — proven-failure red with ✕ — and is used to announce a feed loss, which the spine insists is not a failure.

DESIGN.md · Transient announcement; EXPERIENCE.md · State Patterns, Feed lost
A dropped subscription “proves nothing whatsoever about the payment — the money may well have moved,” yet it is announced in red with a failure cross, in the biggest single-line statement the console has, at the moment 250 people are looking for a verdict. The console contradicts itself on screen: the payment rows behind the line correctly turn neutral grey while the line above them says ✕. “The operator's own read-aloud instinct will follow the red.”
Resolution (applied): the announcement takes two forms — a refusal (red) and a neutral notice (state-neutral), because a lost feed is not a failure. Existing tokens reused; no new colour added to either palette.
high

H7 — A refusal the console produced itself may have no durable record, and the screen it clears into is a large green SETTLED card.

EXPERIENCE.md · Transient announcement, Information Architecture; wireframe S10
“It never carries a fact that is not already durable” is the entire justification for removing the persistent evidence strip — and it was asserted, not specified. Confirmed in code: the console's refusal paths return through RejectedPayment without writing an Evidence entry, so those messages exist only on the line this pass deletes. After the transient clears, the screen is byte-identical to a successful submission, the strip is collapsed because nothing is open, and “the operator narrates a payment the system never received.”
Resolution (applied): stated as a spine requirement that every refusal or failure the console produces itself is recorded in the Evidence workspace before the transient announces it. The transient is never the only record. Removing the bottom band is explicitly conditional on this.
