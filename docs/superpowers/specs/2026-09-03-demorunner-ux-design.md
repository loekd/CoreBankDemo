## Brand & Style

This is a **Terminal.Gui** cockpit, not a web or mobile surface — there is no CSS box model, no font-loading, no hover state, and color rendering depends entirely on the host terminal's capability (24-bit truecolor down to a 16-color ANSI palette). Every token below is written as a hex value for a truecolor terminal; the **Colors** section states the 16-color fallback mapping in prose, because the frontmatter spec fixes color values to hex strings.

The retired `mission-critical-talk-v7.json` cue player leaned on the visual language of the author's deck — a restrained teal-on-navy control-room palette. That posture is preserved **only** where it does not compete with the harder requirement: legibility at projector distance and truthful state that never depends on color perception alone. Where the two pull apart, legibility and text/symbol redundancy win. `[ASSUMPTION]` The exact teal/navy hex values below are a reasonable interpretation of "restrained cockpit language," not values confirmed against the actual deck. The console now ships **two palettes**, not one. The dark cockpit surface remains the default and the brand identity; a light palette exists because the same near-black canvas that reads as a control room on a laptop washes out under a projector lamp, and legibility at projector distance is the harder requirement this spine already commits to. This supersedes this file's earlier assumption that no dark/light split would be modeled. Both palettes are held to the same accessibility floor, and the split changes token *values* only — no component, state, symbol, or behaviour differs between modes.

**Composition reference:** operator-console-inspiration.png (removed in the 2026-09-10 migration; see git history) is a **user-supplied operator-console composition reference** (not a Docker Sandboxes screenshot — see `.memlog.md`'s rename decision), used **only** for the composition principles listed once in `EXPERIENCE.md` Inspiration & Anti-patterns (cross-referenced from here rather than restated). None of its branding, exact colors, labels, or literal layout are carried over; every principle is re-expressed in this console's own restrained teal/navy cockpit language and Terminal.Gui constraints. Where the reference and this spine's stated requirements conflict, **this spine (and its paired `EXPERIENCE.md`) wins**.

## Colors

- **Surface Base (`{colors.surface-base}`)** — the console's canvas: deep navy, near-black. It is now the **single, uninterrupted work surface** — topology bar, every workspace's content, and the fixed lower action region that Resources, Faults and Load Test keep all sit on this one tone, divided only by hairline rules, never by a separate raised "panel" background. Never used for text.
- **Surface Raised (`{colors.surface-raised}`)** — one step up in tone from the base, deliberately reserved for exactly one region: the persistent navigation rail and its item states. That restriction is what makes the rail read as *chrome you navigate with*, distinct at a glance from the near-black canvas you read and act on — it is no longer used for boxed workspace panels. **Surface Overlay (`{colors.surface-overlay}`)** — two steps up, used only for the confirmation modal. Terminal has no shadow, so tone-stepping is the only depth cue (see **Elevation & Depth**).
- **Text Primary (`{colors.text-primary}`)**, **Text Secondary (`{colors.text-secondary}`)**, **Text Muted (`{colors.text-muted}`)** — a three-step readability ramp: primary content, secondary/meta content (timestamps, ids), and disabled/inactive labels. All three clear WCAG AA normal-text contrast (≥4.5:1) against both `surface-base` and `surface-raised`, the only two backgrounds any of the three is rendered on — this is the accessibility floor's non-negotiable line (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Accessibility Floor). Computed ratios: `text-primary` 15.78:1 on `surface-base`, 13.75:1 on `surface-raised`; `text-secondary` 7.66:1 on `surface-base`, 6.67:1 on `surface-raised`; `text-muted` 5.45:1 on `surface-base`, 4.75:1 on `surface-raised`. `text-muted`'s hex was raised from an earlier `#5B6B80` (which measured 3.44:1/3.00:1 and failed AA) to `#7C8CA0` specifically to clear the 4.5:1 floor on `surface-raised`, the tighter of the two backgrounds, while staying visibly dimmer than `text-secondary`.
- **Border Hairline (`{colors.border-hairline}`)** — the low-emphasis divider used by the topology bar, the navigation rail, and the captioned rules that close the Operations compose bar, open the still-open strip, and head the burst takeover. It separates persistent chrome and stacked regions without boxing either into panels; it never carries state. It formerly also drew the evidence strip's top rule; that strip no longer exists (see Components).
- **Accent Teal (`{colors.accent-teal}`)** — the single restrained chromatic accent inherited from the deck's cockpit language. It has exactly **three** roles and no others: the primary action button, the focused-control indicator, and the **lock-exempt / faults-in-force signature** (see below). Never used for a pass/fail state badge, never for chrome, never decoratively — this mirrors the "one accent, used sparingly" discipline that other DESIGN.md examples in this project's toolchain use, adapted here to a control surface rather than a writing surface. `surface-base`-on-`accent-teal` (the primary-action-button's own text/fill pairing) is 7.54:1, well clear of AA; `accent-teal` as a foreground on `surface-base` (fault chip, lock-exempt outlines, slider live fill) is the same 7.54:1, since contrast is symmetric.
  - **Why faults get the third role.** Fault injection is neither a proven pass, a proven failure, nor an awaited proof, so it cannot borrow the `state-*` palette without lying: green would assert a health nothing measured, red a failure that has not happened, amber an outcome still pending. Teal is the only vocabulary left that says *"this is a live thing you did"* rather than *"this is a verdict the system reached."* The same reasoning gives teal to the lock-exempt controls (burst Cancel, fault sliders, panic-off): those, too, state what the operator may do right now, never what the system proved.
- **Fault severity is deliberately colorless.** No token maps to "how bad" a fault level is, and none should be added. Severity is carried by the numeric value, the bar fill length, and the text label; the only thing color says about faults is the binary in-force-or-not. A green-amber-red severity ramp would collide head-on with the `state-*` meanings above — "5% errors" rendered green would read as a proven-healthy badge to every operator who has learned this palette anywhere else in the console.
- **Accent Navy (`{colors.accent-navy}`)** — the structural/secondary accent: active navigation-rail item background, topology-bar structural elements. Distinct from `accent-teal` so "the thing you can press right now" (teal) is never visually confused with "the section you are currently in" (navy). `text-primary`-on-`accent-navy` (the active nav-rail-item label) is 5.62:1; the token's original `#3B6EA5` measured only 4.47:1 there and was darkened to `#325F8C` to clear AA with headroom.
- **State colors — `state-healthy`, `state-running`, `state-neutral`, `state-ambiguous`, `state-failed`, `state-failed-on-raised`.** These carry all pass/fail/unknown meaning in the console and are the one place this DESIGN.md is load-bearing rather than decorative:
  - **`state-failed` (red) is reserved for a proven failure** — an assertion that ran and did not pass, an HTTP error, a resource transition Aspire itself reports as failed. It is never used speculatively. On `surface-base` (status chips, activity-row gutters) it measures 4.73:1, clearing AA.
  - **`state-failed-on-raised`** is a lightened tint (`#E06862`, 4.91:1 on `surface-raised`) used *only* for the destructive-action-button's label, because the base `state-failed` hex measures a sub-AA 4.12:1 on `surface-raised` — not close enough to round up. Both reds read as the same "danger" hue at a glance; only the on-`surface-raised` pairing uses the lightened tint, and only because that is the one place `state-failed` sits on `surface-raised` as normal-weight text that a presenter must not misread.
  - **`state-healthy` (green) is reserved for a proven pass or a confirmed-healthy resource** — never for "probably fine" or "still waiting." 7.88:1 on `surface-base`.
  - **`state-running` and `state-ambiguous` (the same amber)** cover every in-between truth: in flight, awaiting reconciliation, `202 Pending`. Collapsing "running" and "ambiguous" into one amber family is deliberate: both mean "no proof yet," and the accompanying text/symbol (see below) is what distinguishes them, not the color. 8.61:1 on `surface-base`.
  - **`state-neutral` (the same tone as `text-secondary`)** marks resources whose state has not yet been read (cold start, stale snapshot) — genuinely unknown, not merely pending.
  - Every one of these is paired with a fixed symbol (`●` healthy, `~` running/ambiguous, `○` unknown, `✕` failed) and a text label in every rendering; **color is never the sole channel**, satisfying 16-color and color-vision-deficient terminals alike. The running/ambiguous symbol changed from the earlier `◐` (U+25D0, half-filled circle) to the ASCII `~` because `◐` renders inconsistently across monospace terminal fonts (some substitute a box/tofu glyph) and is the hardest of the four to distinguish at projector distance, while `~` is a plain ASCII character (0x7E) guaranteed to render identically everywhere — and because this is explicitly the state the brief cares most about getting right ("nine seconds must look like it is working, not like it hung"), the text label (`Running`/`Ambiguous`/`Pending`) remains the *primary* discriminator in every rendering; the symbol is secondary reinforcement, never the sole cue, for this state specifically. **Monochrome/ASCII-only fallback:** on a terminal that cannot render any of the four symbols (a legacy ASCII-only console, no Unicode box-drawing support), the resolver substitutes bracketed ASCII markers — `[#]` healthy, `[~]` running/ambiguous, `[ ]` unknown, `[X]` failed — with the text label unchanged and still primary; `~` alone needs no such fallback since it is already ASCII.
  - **Fault chip** (`{components.status-chip-fault}`) is a sixth, independent chip — not a pass/fail state — always present in the topology bar. It has three states, each rendered as symbol + label + color and never color alone. `!`/`accent-teal` labeled `Faults in force` means a level is applied *and* has been observed in traffic; `·`/`text-secondary` labeled `Armed` means a Dev Proxy is running but every knob is zero, because an armed proxy injecting nothing must never look like an active fault; `-`/`text-muted` labeled `Unavailable` means this topology started unarmed, or is Attached. The symbol set is plain ASCII (`!`, `·` U+00B7 with `.` as its ASCII fallback, `-`) rather than the earlier `⚡`, which is an emoji-class glyph that renders at inconsistent width across monospace terminals and frequently as tofu — the same reasoning that moved the running state from `◐` to `~`. See Components, Topology bar.
- **Feed status is inline** (`{components.feed-status-inline}`) — `foreground-listening` (`text-muted`) at the foot of the Operations content area, on the burst takeover's captioned rule, and on the Evidence feed header and its awaiting rows, never a chip and never a seventh chip family in the topology bar (placement and its rationale: `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture). A lost feed is rendered by the row changing state, not by adding a badge: `Outcome unknown` takes `foreground-lost` (`state-neutral`/`○`), the palette's existing "genuinely not known" tone.
  - **Why `Outcome unknown` is not red.** `state-failed` is reserved for a proven failure, and a dropped subscription proves nothing whatsoever about the payment — the money may well have moved. Red would assert a payment failure the console has no evidence for, which is the exact error this capability exists to prevent. Gray is not a softer red here; it is the accurate answer.
- **No new color token exists for settlement, and none for cancellation either.** Settled reuses `state-healthy`, a broadcast rejection `state-failed`, and a payment still awaiting its event `state-running` — the same amber that already means `202 Pending`. A payment the bank withdrew before execution reuses **`state-neutral`**.
  - **Why a cancelled payment is not red — nor green, nor amber.** `state-failed` is reserved for a proven failure, and a withdrawal proves nothing failed: the operator asked for the payment to stop, the bank agreed, and no money moved. Red would report a failure the system never had — the same error the `Outcome unknown` rule exists to prevent — and by that rule's own words, gray is not a softer red here; it is the accurate answer when nothing was proven wrong. `state-healthy` is wrong in the other direction: green asserts a proven pass, and a withdrawn payment settled nothing. `state-running`/`state-ambiguous` is wrong too, because the outcome is final and awaits nothing. That leaves `state-neutral`, which already carries *resolved, with no verdict against the system* in this palette, and it is reached by elimination rather than by invention — the fourth member of the outcome contract costs this file no fourth token. The two neutral readings are not confusable, because colour is not what separates them: `⊘ CANCELLED` and `○ Outcome unknown` differ in symbol and in word, exactly as the never-colour-alone rule requires. `[ASSUMPTION]` `⊘` (U+2298) has not been rendered against the target terminal fonts; on the same reasoning that demoted `◐` to `~`, the word `CANCELLED` stays the primary discriminator and the monochrome/ASCII fallback marker is `[/]`, beside the existing `[#]`/`[~]`/`[ ]`/`[X]`.
  - A cancel the bank refused changes nothing about what has been proven, and a refusal is never read as a claim that the row is executing: while the status the answer carries is non-terminal the payment is still awaiting and keeps `state-running`, and where that status is terminal the payment takes that outcome's own token, exactly as if it had arrived by broadcast (see `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns, *Payment: Cancel refused (`409`)*). A cancel that arrived too late renders as the outcome that actually happened — `state-healthy` and its balance legs, or `state-failed` and its reason — never as a cancellation that did not occur.
- **16-color fallback.** On a terminal without truecolor, the resolver maps: `state-failed`/`state-failed-on-raised`→ANSI Red, `state-healthy`→ANSI Green, `state-running`/`state-ambiguous`→ANSI Yellow, `accent-teal`→ANSI Cyan, `accent-navy`→ANSI Blue, `text-primary`→ANSI White/Bright White, `text-secondary`/`text-muted`→ANSI White with the `Dim` attribute (Terminal.Gui has no separate gray in the base 16), `surface-base`/`surface-raised`/`surface-overlay`→ANSI Black/Bright Black via the `Bold`/background-intensity bit. `[ASSUMPTION]` This mapping has not been rendered and screenshotted against a real 16-color terminal; it is the designer's best-effort interpretation of the nearest ANSI neighbor for each hex value.
- **Avoid:** gradients (Terminal.Gui cannot render them), a second chromatic accent beyond teal/navy, and any state communicated by hue alone.

### Light mode

A second palette (`colors-light` in the frontmatter), for presenting on a projector. It is selected with `--light`, with `COREBANK_DEMO_THEME=light`, or by pressing `T` while the console is running; dark remains the default. Behaviour is defined in `EXPERIENCE.md` Interaction Primitives.

**Light mode is a different set of hexes, not the dark set on a pale background.** This was measured rather than assumed. Every dark token above, placed as a foreground on a white canvas, contrasts as follows: `state-running` 2.17:1, `state-healthy` 2.37:1, `accent-teal` 2.48:1, `text-secondary` 2.44:1, `text-muted` 3.43:1, `state-failed` 3.96:1, `text-primary` 1.19:1. Eight of the ten fail the 4.5:1 floor and the amber, green and teal are effectively invisible at projector distance. That is the expected result for a palette tuned light-on-dark, and it is why the light mode carries its own values.

**Anchored to the host terminal.** The light surfaces are taken from the Ghostty **GitHub Light Default** theme this console is presented in: `surface-base` is that theme's exact background (`#FFFFFF`) and `text-primary` its exact foreground (`#1F2328`). The state and accent hues are drawn from the same theme's ANSI palette (GitHub Primer), which is itself contrast-tuned for a light terminal. Two consequences worth stating: the console does not read as a foreign rectangle inside the terminal hosting it, and the 16-colour fallback below degrades to *the same colours* rather than to approximations of them.

**Measured contrast, light mode.** Every value clears WCAG AA (4.5:1) on both backgrounds it is drawn on, and every state and accent token clears 6:1 on the canvas — the reading that matters from the back of a room:

| token | hex | on `surface-base` | on `surface-raised` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `text-primary` | `#1F2328` | 17.53:1 | 15.46:1 |
| `text-secondary` / `state-neutral` | `#57606A` | 7.52:1 | 6.63:1 |
| `text-muted` | `#5F6873` | 5.65:1 | 4.85:1 |
| `accent-teal` | `#136066` | 7.26:1 | 6.23:1 |
| `accent-navy` | `#325F8C` | 6.66:1 | 5.88:1 |
| `state-healthy` | `#116329` | 7.39:1 | 6.34:1 |
| `state-running` / `state-ambiguous` | `#7A5309` | 6.84:1 | 5.87:1 |
| `state-failed` | `#A40E26` | 7.87:1 | 6.75:1 |

Notes on the deviations from a straight mirror of the dark palette:

- **`accent-navy` is the single token identical in both modes.** At `#325F8C` it measures 5.62:1 against the dark `text-primary` and 6.66:1 against white, so it clears the floor from both directions. Keeping one fixed structural accent is deliberate: it is the token that says *which section you are in*, and that meaning should not shift with the lighting in the room.
- **`state-failed-on-raised` collapses onto `state-failed` in light mode.** The dark palette needs a lightened red (`#E06862`) because the base red measures a sub-AA 4.12:1 on `surface-raised`. The light red measures 6.75:1 there, so no separate tint is warranted and inventing one would be decoration. The token is retained so both palettes fill the same shape and no component has to know which mode is in force.
- **`text-muted` is `#5F6873`, not the theme's own `#6E7781`.** The theme value measures 3.90:1 on `surface-raised` and fails; `#5F6873` reaches 4.85:1 while staying visibly dimmer than `text-secondary`. This is the same adjustment, for the same reason, that raised the dark palette's `text-muted` from `#5B6B80` to `#7C8CA0`, and it lands at effectively the same floor (4.85:1 light, 4.75:1 dark).
- **Amber is darkened hardest.** `state-running` goes to `#7A5309`, a deep ochre rather than the dark palette's bright `#D8A93B`. Amber is the worst-behaved hue on white, and this is the state the brief cares most about reading correctly ("nine seconds must look like it is working, not like it hung"). Its identity survives because, per the rule above, the *text label* is the primary discriminator for this state and the colour was never carrying it alone.
- **`text-muted` on `surface-overlay`** measures 4.19:1 and is below the floor — exactly as the dark palette's equivalent pairing measures 4.21:1. Neither mode renders muted text on the overlay: the confirmation modal uses `text-primary` only. The constraint is identical in both modes, so light mode introduces no accessibility debt the dark mode did not already carry.

**16-colour fallback, light mode.** `state-failed` → ANSI Red, `state-healthy` → ANSI Green, `state-running`/`state-ambiguous` → ANSI Yellow, `accent-teal` → ANSI Cyan, `accent-navy` → ANSI Blue, `text-primary` → ANSI Black, `text-secondary`/`text-muted` → ANSI Bright Black, `surface-base`/`surface-raised`/`surface-overlay` → ANSI White/Bright White. Unlike the dark mapping, this one is not an approximation when the host terminal is GitHub Light Default: the palette above *is* that terminal's ANSI set, so a degraded terminal renders very nearly the same colours.

## Typography

Terminal.Gui has no font family or size control — the host terminal emulator owns the actual glyph rendering, always monospace. "Typography" here means **attribute roles**, not font choices: `label` (bold) for headers/captions/primary-action text, `body` (normal) for the bulk of tables and forms, `meta` (dim) for timestamps/ids/footnotes, and `evidence-mono` (unstyled, never truncated or silently re-wrapped — pannable by default, with an explicit opt-in wrap toggle) for raw HTTP/JSON bodies and log lines where byte-for-byte fidelity matters more than compactness. `[ASSUMPTION]` "Projector legibility" for typography is delegated to the operator's terminal font-size setting outside this console's control; the console's own obligation is to never rely on a text size distinction to convey meaning (for example, no "small print" disclaimers) and to keep line lengths compatible with a compact/narrow terminal (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Responsive & Platform). `[ASSUMPTION]` A recommended projector baseline — legible from the back of a ~250-seat room — is 100×30 cells at roughly 18–20pt monospace; this is a starting point for dress rehearsal, not a verified claim, and is promoted to tested fact only after a real-terminal rehearsal (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Projector mode).

**Emphasis without a size ramp — the focus card's state word.** The Operations focus card makes one payment the largest semantic object on the screen, and a terminal offers exactly one font size in which to do it. Four channels carry that emphasis instead, all of them already in this file's vocabulary and none of them colour. **Attribute:** the state word takes the `label` role (bold), while the request detail and balance legs beneath it are `body` and the clock, transaction id, key and submit-time stamp are `meta` — the card's own three-step ramp, matching the console's. **Case:** the card upper-cases the state (`SETTLED`, `AWAITING SETTLEMENT`, `CANCELLED BY THE RAIL`) where the same word in the still-open strip below is title case (`Awaiting`), so one vocabulary renders at two weights of importance without a second type size. **Position:** the word always begins in the same cell — the first after `{spacing.focus-card-symbol-gutter}`, on the region's first line — so it is the first thing read and never moves as the detail below it changes. **Whitespace:** that first line is the only line in the region with a blank row above and below it; in a fixed grid, reserved empty cells are the only scale available, and the redesign spends its recovered rows partly on them. Colour is a fifth channel, but never the emphasis one: it says *which* state, never *how important*, and the word reads identically on a monochrome terminal (see Do's and Don'ts).

## Layout & Spacing

The spacing scale is in terminal cells (`ch`/`row`), not pixels: `{spacing.0}` where a region abuts its neighbour with no padding at all, and `{spacing.1}`, `{spacing.2}` and `{spacing.3}` for internal padding, `{spacing.gutter}` between adjacent regions, `{spacing.pane-margin}` around the outer frame, `{spacing.section-gap}` between stacked sections, `{spacing.nav-rail-width}` as the fixed compact width of the navigation rail. The persistent topology bar (top) and navigation rail (left) are fixed-size (their content is bounded and truncates gracefully, never wraps unpredictably); everything else — the active workspace's content, down to the fixed lower action region that Resources, Faults and Load Test still keep — fills the remaining space as one continuous surface, not a set of boxed sub-panels. **The shell no longer carries a bottom band at all**: workspace content now runs to the last row of the frame (see Components, *Evidence strip — removed*).

**The navigation rail is `{spacing.nav-rail-width}` — 16 cells, down from 22.** That number is derived rather than chosen: the longest of the five workspace labels, `Load Test`, together with its active marker and one-key shortcut, needs 14 cells, and the rail's divider and margin need 2. Everything beyond that was padding, and padding in a persistent rail is paid for by every workspace at once — the six cells it gave back are six more for a payment's account identifiers, a fault's value column, or an evidence line that would otherwise have to pan. The rail still holds a *fixed* width rather than flexing with its content, because a rail that resized as labels changed would move the workspace's left edge under the reader mid-sentence; it simply stopped reserving more than its longest label needs. This supersedes the earlier `14ch`, which counted the label column alone and left the rail's real occupied width unstated at 22. At `{spacing.preferred-width}` the workspace's inner content line is therefore 80 cells, and at `{spacing.hard-min-width}` with the compact rail (`{spacing.nav-rail-width-compact}` plus its divider, 5 cells) it is 71.

The Operations workspace stacks three regions on that surface — compose bar, focus card, still-open strip — and adds the tokens that stop them sliding under each other. The compose bar is `{spacing.compose-bar-height}` tall and grows to `{spacing.compose-bar-height-max}` only in Supplied mode (the supplied-key field) or Omitted mode (the not-retry-safe warning); both of its lines end in a right-anchored `{spacing.compose-bar-action-slot}`, so Submit and Burst… stack in one column against the right margin. The focus card reserves `{spacing.focus-card-symbol-gutter}` for the state symbol, `{spacing.focus-card-state-column}` for the state word — 34 cells, sized to `CANCELLED BY THE RAIL`, the longest of the seven and the only one that names *who* withdrew a payment rather than only that it was withdrawn — `{spacing.elapsed-clock-column}` for the right-aligned elapsed-or-final clock, `{spacing.focus-card-action-slot}` for the payment's own action against that same right margin, and `{spacing.submit-time-column}` on its meta line for that payment's own wall-clock submit time. All five are fixed rather than content-sized, which is the point: the clock and the action must not shuffle sideways as `AWAITING SETTLEMENT` resolves to `SETTLED`, and the action slot is sized to the longest label it will ever hold (`[ Look up outcome ]`, 19 cells plus the one-cell margin) so `[ Cancel payment ]` and `[ Resend same key ]` land where the eye already is. The still-open strip adds only `{spacing.still-open-selection-gutter}` — the two cells left of the ordinary status gutter that carry its selection marker — and reuses `{spacing.elapsed-clock-column}`, so an elapsed clock is the same shape in the strip as on the card. **It has no height token because it has no fixed height:** one row per open payment while more than one is open, and nothing whatsoever — its rule included — otherwise. The card prints account identifiers in full; the strip truncates them to their last four digits, because the full identity of any listed payment is one keypress away in the card above it.

Those column widths are measured off the approved Stage-focus wireframes ([`wireframes/operations-stage-focus.md`](2026-09-03-demorunner-operations-stage-focus-wireframe-design.md)), which are drawn at the real content line: every frame there is 82 cells wide with 80 usable inside it, and the floor screen 73 with 71 inside — exactly the lines the rail at `{spacing.nav-rail-width}` leaves at `{spacing.preferred-width}` and at `{spacing.hard-min-width}`. **This supersedes this file's earlier claim that the drawings were made against a 74-cell line and under-drew the real one by six cells**: that described an earlier, superseded set of drawings and has not described the linked file since it was redrawn. Every token above is therefore a *reserved* width, anchored to its region's right edge or left gutter and measured against those same 80- and 71-cell lines; what flexes is the middle of each line — the account fields, the request detail, the strip's truncated identifiers — and never a reserved column.

The Faults workspace adds three width tokens: `{spacing.fault-slider-track-width}` is the preferred bar width, `{spacing.fault-slider-track-min}` the narrowest bar still worth drawing, and `{spacing.fault-value-column}` the fixed column that holds the printed level and its staged delta. The value column is **never** sacrificed to preserve the track — the number is authoritative and the bar is reinforcement, so degradation removes the bar first and the number never (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Responsive & Platform).

The burst takeover adds four width tokens of its own: `{spacing.burst-counter-label-column}` holds the `Sent`/`Settled` line labels and `{spacing.burst-counter-value-column}` the figure that follows each — both fixed, so the two lines' numbers align vertically and the room compares them by looking straight down — and `{spacing.burst-bar-track-width}` is the proportional bar beneath them, with `{spacing.burst-bar-caption-column}` the fixed column that holds that bar's caption in the cells to its right. The bar follows the fault slider's rule exactly: the printed numbers are authoritative and the bar is reinforcement, so a terminal too narrow to draw the track loses the track whole and never a count (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Responsive & Platform).

**Concrete thresholds** (full behavior/rationale in `EXPERIENCE.md` Responsive & Platform): `[ASSUMPTION]` the preferred baseline is `{spacing.preferred-width}` × `{spacing.preferred-height}` (100 cols × 30 rows) — chosen as a reasonable authoring target for this brief, not yet confirmed against a real terminal/projector rehearsal (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Projector mode). At that baseline the shell now spends `{spacing.shell-chrome-height}` — 7 of the 30 rows — on chrome, where it spent 10 before: removing the three-row bottom band from every workspace hands those three rows back to content, and nothing else about the chrome changed. Of the 23 rows that remain in Operations, 3 go to the compose bar and its rule and 20 to the payment area. There the focus card takes the rows its content needs, and the still-open strip takes one row per open payment while more than one is open — and no rows whatsoever, its rule included, when fewer are. The last row carries the feed status: right-aligned when the strip is collapsed, folded into the strip's own captioned rule when it is not. Nothing in that area is a reserved band: a transient announcement, while one is showing, occupies the row immediately above the feed status rather than a slot held permanently empty for it. Below that baseline, the shell enters **compact** mode: the navigation rail shrinks to `{spacing.nav-rail-width-compact}` (icon+shortcut only, labels hidden — 5 cells with its divider, which is what leaves a 71-cell content line at `{spacing.hard-min-width}`) and workspace content becomes a single full-view pane instead of a side-by-side layout. `{spacing.hard-min-width}` × `{spacing.hard-min-height}` (80 cols × 24 rows) is the hard minimum; below it the console shows a non-blocking size hint but never crashes or loses state. The topology bar's resource-chip strip never hides a resource: chips abbreviate their label to a fixed 3-character code before any chip would be dropped, and a chip is never fully hidden regardless of terminal width.

## Elevation & Depth

Terminal.Gui has no shadow or blur primitive, so depth is expressed only through **tone-stepping** (`surface-base` → `surface-raised` → `surface-overlay`) and **border weight**. The console is deliberately shallow and deliberately flat: the topology bar, every workspace's content, and the fixed lower action region Resources, Faults and Load Test keep all sit on the same `surface-base` tone — one uninterrupted near-black work surface, separated only by hairline rules, never by a raised "panel" background. The navigation rail is the single exception, one tone up on `surface-raised`, so it reads as chrome rather than content. Exactly one layer — the confirmation modal — sits above everything on `surface-overlay` with a `surface-base` scrim implied behind it (rendered as a dimmed/unfocused background, since Terminal.Gui has no true alpha blending). No second modal ever stacks on the first; a confirmation is answered or cancelled before anything else can happen, consistent with the single-action-in-flight rule.

Two surfaces the Operations redesign introduces are deliberately **not** layers. The **burst takeover** replaces the workspace's content in place — same `surface-base` tone, no scrim, no border box, no tone step: it is a different thing to read in the same place, not something floating over what it hides, and a takeover that dressed itself as an overlay would imply a screen still underneath that the operator could see past. The **transient announcement** likewise takes a row of the content area at `surface-base` rather than an overlay tone, because a line that will clear must not look weightier than the durable record that outlives it. Exactly one thing in this console ever steps up to `surface-overlay`, and it is still the confirmation modal.

## Shapes

There is no corner radius in a terminal — "shape" is expressed through **box-drawing border style** instead, which is why this spine omits the `rounded` frontmatter token in favor of a `border` field on each component. Two border weights exist: **single-line hairline** for calm, reversible dividers (topology bar's bottom rule, navigation rail's right rule, the rule that closes the Operations compose bar, the rule that opens the still-open strip) — never a full box around workspace content — and **double-line** reserved exclusively for the confirmation modal, so a destructive action's confirmation is visually distinct from every read-only or already-confirmed surface before the operator reads a word of it. The **captioned rule** is that same single weight with its left end interrupted by a `label`-typography caption (`STILL OPEN ────`, `BURST · 200 instant payments ────`) and its right end able to carry a `meta` qualifier (the feed status, a run clock). It is a third *use* of the single weight, not a third weight: a caption never promotes a rule to a box, and a region a captioned rule opens is still part of the same continuous surface. `[ASSUMPTION]` This two-weight scheme is a deliberate adaptation of the "shapes" concept to a non-CSS platform, not a literal instruction from any source document.

## Components

Every component below is paired with a same-named **Component Patterns** row in `EXPERIENCE.md` (behavioral rules there; visual tokens here) — see that file for the behavior each visual spec supports. Two entries are not components and carry no such row: the cross-cutting **Lock-exempt control signature**, and the note recording the removed **Evidence strip**.

- **Topology bar** (`{components.topology-bar}`) — persistent, one row, top of shell, on `surface-base` like the canvas beneath it. Shows the current AppHost profile (Regular/LoadTests), Owned/Attached badge, the persistent fault chip (`{components.status-chip-fault}`), and a horizontal strip of resource status chips. Never scrolls out of view. Refresh cadence, hysteresis, and immediate command-state behavior are defined in `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns.
- **Navigation rail** (`{components.nav-rail}`) — persistent, compact, fixed-width (`{spacing.nav-rail-width}`) column on the left edge of the shell, `surface-raised` with a single hairline-right divider — 16 cells, the longest of the five labels plus its border and not one more (see **Layout & Spacing**). Hosts one **nav rail item** (`{components.nav-rail-item}`) per workspace — marked active by a leading `▸` glyph (`marker-active`), optionally reinforced by an `accent-navy` fill, with `surface-raised` and a leading space when inactive — each reachable by a single keypress (`1`–`5`) or click; never nested, never a horizontal tab strip. **The marker, not the fill, is what carries the active state**, so which workspace you are in survives a monochrome terminal and both palettes identically; the fill is reinforcement. An active item's label takes `foreground-active` (`{colors.text-on-accent}`), never `text-primary`: `text-primary` inverts between modes while `accent-navy` deliberately does not, so a straight token swap would put dark text on mid-navy at 2.37:1 in light mode. Below `{spacing.preferred-width}`, shrinks to `{spacing.nav-rail-width-compact}` (icon+shortcut only, labels hidden).
- **Status chip** (`{components.status-chip-healthy}` / `{components.status-chip-running}` / `{components.status-chip-unknown}` / `{components.status-chip-failed}`) — symbol + short label + color, per the **Colors** rule that color is never the sole channel. Used in the topology bar and, per **Activity row** below, in the left-hand status gutter of every activity/resource row. Each chip is an individually focusable/Tab-reachable control (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Accessibility Floor for the literal focus order); Enter/click on a resource chip opens that resource's detail in the Resources workspace.
- **Feed status, inline** (`{components.feed-status-inline}`) — not a component with a fixed screen position but a `meta`-typography qualifier that travels with the thing it qualifies. It appears in four places and no fifth. Once at the foot of the Operations content area (`listening since 14:02:11`, `foreground-listening`) — right-aligned on the last row when the still-open strip is collapsed, and carried on the right end of that strip's captioned rule when it is not. Once on the right end of the **Burst takeover**'s captioned rule, which is the same statement travelling with the surface that replaces both of those placements. As the Evidence feed's header line (`Listening since 12:01:04 — events before this time were not observed`). And inline on an Evidence awaiting row, which keeps the per-row qualifier the override below removes in Operations alone. **This supersedes the earlier placement in Operations**, which parenthesised the qualifier on every awaiting payment row's own elapsed readout (`Awaiting settlement — 14s (listening)`): the Stage-focus workspace renders one payment large and the open remainder one line each, so a per-row parenthetical would either repeat down the list or appear on a single row and read as being about that row. One line at the region's foot states it once for everything above it, which is the scope it always had — and Evidence/Results, whose rows are a record rather than a live queue, keeps its inline form. It is never a chip, and never a seventh chip family in the topology bar; when the feed is lost the payment rows change **state** rather than gaining a badge: it renders `symbol-lost` (`○`, ASCII fallback `[ ]`) in `foreground-lost` (`state-neutral`), exactly like every other genuinely-unknown state in this console (see `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns, Payment: Outcome unknown).
- **Activity row** (`{components.activity-row}`) — the atomic unit of the three row-list workspaces' main content — Resources, Evidence/Results and Load Test (resource rows, evidence entries, invariant chips). Faults renders a fixed set of labelled controls and Operations the three stacked Stage-focus regions above; both obey every rule below except being lists of these rows. The row is: a bold verb/object headline (`label` typography, `foreground-headline`) — for example, "Stop — corebank-api" — with a muted command/request detail line directly beneath it (`meta` typography, `foreground-meta`) — for example, the exact Aspire command or HTTP call issued — and a fixed-width status marker rendered in a left gutter (`gutter-width`) column, never inline-mixed with the headline text. Sits directly on `surface-base`, borderless, so a scrolling list of rows reads as one continuous surface rather than stacked cards. An empty workspace renders a single muted (`text-muted`) placeholder row in the same slot — for example, "No payments submitted this session" — never a blank void. The Operations **Still-open strip** is the one scoped exception to that rule, for the reason given there.
- **Resource row** (`{components.resource-row}`) — an **Activity row** specialization: fixed `action-slot-width` at the row's right edge always shows the single legal next command (Start/Stop/Restart) for that resource in the exact same column position regardless of which label is showing, so the recovery action lands exactly where the prior action was. During a commanded transition, the row's status marker shows `status-chip-running`/`~` plus an elapsed-time readout in the `meta` line — for example, "Restarting — 4s."
- **Event row** (`{components.event-row}`) — an **Activity row** specialization for a record the *system* produced rather than one the operator commanded, used in the Evidence/Results event feed and, condensed, as the closing block of the Operations focus card. Its primary distinction is non-chromatic: an `inbound-gutter-width` column left of the normal status gutter carries the `inbound-marker` (`<`), so a scan down the Evidence list tells "what the operator did" from "what the bank said" without reading a word. The CloudEvent type prints in `foreground-event-type` (`text-muted`), and a settled row's balance legs occupy a fixed `settlement-leg-column`, so two legs line up vertically down the feed and a missing leg reads as an empty column rather than an absence the eye skips. Two states do carry colour: an unmatched event's headline takes `foreground-unattributed` (`text-muted`), and one contradicting a proven HTTP outcome takes `foreground-contradiction` (`state-failed`). Headline grammar, field list, and the `Unattributed`/`Contradicted` semantics are behaviour — see `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns and State Patterns.
- **AppHost control panel** (`{components.apphost-control-panel}`) — `surface-base`, borderless, one row per AppHost profile (Regular/LoadTests). Each row shows the profile name, an Owned (`state-healthy`-tinted badge) or Attached (`state-neutral`-tinted badge) indicator, and up to four action slots — Start/Attach use `primary-action-button` tokens, Stop/Switch use `destructive-action-button` tokens. When the current topology is Attached, the whole-AppHost Stop and Switch slots render in their `destructive-action-button` position but visibly disabled (dimmed to `text-muted`, no focus ring) — never hidden — while per-resource Start/Stop/Restart on that same Attached topology remain live, styled identically to a normal **Resource row**'s action slot, so "this whole-AppHost control is off" and "this one resource control is on" read as two distinct, deliberate visual states rather than one blanket-disabled panel.
- **Compose bar** (`{components.compose-bar}`) — **supersedes the Payment form** of earlier revisions of this file: the same tokens and the same field vocabulary, rendered as a two-line bar at the top of the Operations workspace instead of a sixteen-row column. `surface-base`, borderless, closed by a `rule-below` hairline in `rule-color`. `label`-typography captions (`foreground-label`) sit beside `body`-typography editable values (`foreground-value`): the first line carries `From <iban> → To <iban>` and ends in a right-anchored `action-slot-width` holding Submit, which keeps `action-slot-primary` (`primary-action-button`) tokens; the second carries Amount, the `standard`/`instant` rail as two adjacent chips (`rail-chip-selected`/`rail-chip-unselected`, same visual language as the **Idempotency selector**), the selector's Key chips, and `[ Burst… ]` in that same right-hand column. A third line renders only in Supplied mode (the supplied-key field) or Omitted mode, where it carries the selector's `warning-foreground` (`state-ambiguous`) caption; `height`/`height-max` bound the region either way. **There is no currency field.** A caption beside a value the operator cannot change is chrome that teaches the room nothing, and the settlement legs still print their currency suffix where the figure is real (see **Event row**). What the console puts on the wire is behaviour — see `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns, *Compose bar*. `[ Burst… ]` is deliberately not a second primary action: it takes the object-anchored treatment (`action-slot-secondary-border`/`-foreground`/`-fill` — single border, `text-primary`, no fill), so exactly one filled-teal control exists on the screen.
- **Focus card** (`{components.focus-card}`) — the Operations workspace's one large object: `surface-base`, borderless, no fill of its own. It is a card only in the sense of *one thing given the room's attention*; it is a region on the continuous surface, never a raised panel. Its first line is a fixed grid — `symbol-gutter-width` for the state symbol, `state-column` for the state word in `state-word-attribute` bold and `state-word-case` upper, `clock-column` right-aligned for the elapsed or final clock, `action-slot-width` at the right margin for the payment's own action — so nothing on that line moves as the state resolves. Symbol and word take the state's colour: `foreground-settled` (`state-healthy`), `foreground-rejected` (`state-failed`), `foreground-contradicted` (`state-failed`), `foreground-awaiting` (`state-running`), `foreground-ambiguous` (`state-ambiguous`), `foreground-cancelled` (`state-neutral`), `foreground-unknown` (`state-neutral`) — always symbol + word + colour, never colour alone. Two of those seven are new to the card's list and neither adds a colour. `~ AMBIGUOUS` takes the amber the palette already gives every unproven in-between truth, and takes it rather than `foreground-awaiting`'s identical hex through its own token, so an implementer cannot map it to a payment that is legitimately waiting; the word, not the colour, is what separates them (see **Colors**). `✕ CONTRADICTED` takes the proven-failure red, because a disagreement the console can *prove* between two statements about one payment is itself a proven fact about the system — the same reading the **Event row**'s `foreground-contradiction` already carries, and the one state where the card's closing block renders twice, once per claim with its source and time (see `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns). `symbol-cancelled` (`⊘`) carries `fallback-symbol-cancelled` (`[/]`) for the same monochrome/ASCII terminals the other four markers already have fallbacks for. Below that line, `foreground-detail` (`text-secondary`) carries the request detail and the accounts in full, `foreground-meta` (`text-muted`) the transaction id, the key, and — in `stamp-column` — that payment's own submit time as a wall clock (`14:02:11`), because the clock above it is a duration and a duration cannot say *when*: the resting card holds its payment indefinitely, and a settlement from twenty minutes and one topology ago must not read as one that just happened. The card's last block is whichever truth applies — the lifecycle in words, the balance legs in the **Event row**'s `settlement-leg-column`, or the `ErrorReason` — one such block, and, `foreground-contradicted` excepted, never two. Before the first submission it renders `foreground-placeholder` (`text-muted`) placeholder lines rather than a blank region. **The card is occupied from the instant Submit is pressed, not from the instant an answer arrives**, and the same grid covers that state with no special case: the request as sent in `foreground-detail`, `foreground-awaiting` in the symbol and word, `clock-column` counting up from zero, and the action slot already drawn. That slot holds a live **Cancel payment action**, or — where the console has no id to cancel with — that same action in its `disabled-foreground` (`text-muted`) with `disabled-fill: none`, in place rather than absent, so the slot never sits empty and a disabled action is legible as disabled rather than merely unresponsive. **This supersedes the earlier reading of the card as a surface a response fills**; which submissions can be cancelled, and what the card says while no answer has come, are behaviour (`EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns). **Most of the time the card is the only thing on this surface**: whenever nothing or a single payment is open, the still-open strip renders nothing at all and the card owns the whole payment area, so the card is specified to look finished alone, with its own whitespace above and below its first line, and never as the top half of a layout that expects a list beneath it (rationale: `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture, *Why Operations shows no history*). Emphasis in that state comes from attribute, case, position and whitespace, never from size or colour (see **Typography**).
- **Still-open strip** (`{components.still-open-strip}`) — **supersedes the session-history list** the first Stage-focus drafts placed here. It lists only payments with no proven outcome yet, one compact line each, and it renders at all only while more than one of them is open, opened then by a captioned `rule` (`rule-color`) whose right end carries the inline feed status. Each row is one line, not an **Activity row**'s two: `selection-gutter-width` carrying `marker-selected` (`▸`) or `marker-unselected`, then the ordinary status `gutter-width` with its **Status chip** symbol and title-case label, the amount, the identifiers truncated to their last four digits, the rail, and the elapsed clock in `elapsed-column`. `foreground` (`text-secondary`) carries the row, `foreground-meta` (`text-muted`) its trailing detail — one tone below the card in both palettes, because the card is the object and these are its neighbours. Selection is carried by the `▸` marker rather than a fill, exactly as the **Nav rail item** carries the active workspace, so it survives a monochrome terminal. **Its collapsed state — nothing open, or a single payment open — renders nothing at all** — `empty-height: 0row`, rule included, no placeholder row. **This supersedes the earlier rule that the strip collapsed only with nothing open**, which would have spent a row and a rule restating the one payment the card is already showing at full size. The collapse is a deliberate, scoped exception to the **Activity row** rule that an empty list shows a muted placeholder instead of a blank void, and it is safe here for a reason that does not generalise: the region above is never empty. The focus card always renders something, so *nothing further is open* is already legible as the card standing alone, and a `No payments awaiting` row would spend rows of the payment area restating what the card just said. No other list in this console takes the exception (rationale: `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture, *Why Operations shows no history*).
- **Cancel payment action** (`{components.cancel-payment-action}`) — sits in the focus card's `action-slot-width` and takes the object-anchored treatment: single `border`, `text-primary` label, `fill: none`. It is pointedly three things it might have been and is not. It is **not** a `destructive-action-button` (`destructive-treatment: false`, `confirm-style: none`): the double-bordered red is this console's one data-loss warning, and a cancellation loses no data to warn about — the same reason the **Apply faults action** stays off those tokens. Why the cancellation itself takes no confirmation is argued once in `EXPERIENCE.md` Interaction Primitives and is not repeated here. It is **not** a `primary-action-button`: Submit is Operations' single filled-teal control, and a second would break the one-primary-per-screen rule. And it is **not** given the teal outline of the **Lock-exempt control signature**: this control is exempt from confirmation, not from the lock, and an outline that means *still live while everything else is dimmed* must not be borrowed by a control that is merely attached to the object beneath it. Where the console holds nothing to cancel with, the slot renders the same label in `disabled-foreground` (`text-muted`) with `disabled-fill: none` rather than vanishing, exactly as the **Resend-same-key action** does. `text-primary` on `surface-base` measures 15.78:1 dark and 17.53:1 light, so the treatment needs no token of its own in either palette.
- **Idempotency selector** (`{components.idempotency-selector}`) — a three-chip segmented control (Generated / Supplied / Omitted) using `chip-selected-*`/`chip-unselected-*` tokens identical in kind to the compose bar's rail chips, rendered inline on the compose bar's second line; the Omitted chip additionally renders a `warning-foreground` (`state-ambiguous`) inline caption — "not retry-safe after an ambiguous outcome" — beneath it at all times, not only after submission.
- **Resend-same-key action** (`{components.resend-same-key-action}`) — the payment's own action after a Generated/Supplied submission, so it renders in the focus card's `action-slot-width` in the same object-anchored treatment as Cancel (single `border`, `text-primary`, `fill: none`). **This supersedes its earlier spec**, which gave it `primary-action-button` tokens: with Submit anchored in the compose bar, a filled-teal Resend would be the screen's second primary action, and the one-primary rule is worth more than the emphasis. When disabled (after an Omitted-key submission resolves ambiguously) it renders as `disabled-foreground` (`text-muted`) label text with no fill and no focus ring, in the same screen slot, so its disablement is legible rather than merely non-responsive.
- **Burst control** (`{components.burst-control}`) — `surface-base`, a bounded numeric field (`body` typography) plus live `meta`-typography counters on **two labeled lines**, each counter prefixed with the matching status-chip symbol/color, and a proportional bar beneath them. The lines are **Sent** and **Settled**, renamed from the earlier `HTTP leg` and `proven leg`: those named the mechanism rather than the event and did not survive being read aloud by their own author. **The Sent line carries no state colour at all**: `counter-accepted` and `counter-failed` both render in `text-primary`, for exactly the reason the bar carries none. Sent reports what the API answered, and no answer on that line is a proven outcome for a payment — green on `accepted` would assert a pass the burst has not yet earned, and red on `failed` would assert a rejection where a failed send is the one figure on this screen whose outcome is genuinely unknown. **This supersedes this line's earlier spec**, which gave `counter-accepted` `state-healthy` and `counter-failed` `state-failed`, putting a green figure nothing had proved on the line above the one figure on that screen that had been. Its denominator is always the run's requested count and never re-bases, so `Sent 200 / 200` cannot come to mean anything but *every payment this run asked for*. Only the **Settled** line carries the proven-pass and proven-failure tokens: it reports what the broadcast proved, driven only by arriving events (`counter-settled` reuses `state-healthy`, `counter-rejected` reuses `state-failed`, `counter-awaiting` reuses `state-running` and prints as "still moving", and `counter-unknown` reuses `state-neutral` for the payments the console can no longer account for — the figure that replaces `still moving` the instant the feed drops, since a count of payments nobody is listening for is not a wait; see **Burst takeover**). `label-column` and `value-column` hold each line's label and figure in fixed columns, so the two lines' numbers align vertically and are compared by looking straight down. The two lines are never merged into one row of figures (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns, Burst control). The bar is `bar-track-width` wide, `bar-fill-symbol` (`█`) over `bar-track-symbol` (`░`) with `#`/`.` as their ASCII fallbacks, drawn in `bar-fill` (`text-secondary`) on a `bar-track` (`border-hairline`) track — the fault slider's staged-fill treatment exactly. Its caption occupies `bar-caption-column` in `bar-caption-foreground` (`text-muted`, `meta` typography) in the cells immediately right of the track, and it names its own numerator in full — `188 of 200 settled or rejected`, which fits that column exactly at the 80-cell line. **This supersedes the drawn `187 of 200 proven`**, whose numerator was unstated and coincided with the `Settled` figure directly above it only because every drawn screen has `rejected 0`; the caption must never be readable as a restatement of the line above. The caption is authoritative and the track is reinforcement, so where a line cannot hold both, the track shortens and then goes, and the caption is never abbreviated. **The bar carries no state colour.** It measures how much of the burst has proved itself *either way*, so a green bar would report 187 healthy payments on a figure that counts rejections as equally proven; it reinforces the printed numbers and never replaces them, and it is the first thing dropped when the width is not there (see **Layout & Spacing**). Its **Cancel** control is one of the console's named exemptions from the global single-action-in-flight lock (see `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns): it renders with a single `accent-teal` outline border and no fill (`cancel-fill: none`) — visually distinct from both a normal enabled `primary-action-button` (filled) and a normal disabled control (`text-muted`, no border) — so a presenter can see at a glance that Cancel is still live while every other mutating control is dimmed. This outline-teal-no-fill treatment is now a **shared signature**, not a one-off: see Lock-exempt control signature below.
- **Burst takeover** (`{components.burst-takeover}`) — while it is showing, the burst control's counters and bar are the only thing in the Operations content area: compose bar, focus card and still-open strip are *replaced*, not overlaid (see **Elevation & Depth**). `surface-base`, no border box, no tone step, headed by a captioned `rule` (`rule-color`) whose left end names the run in `foreground-heading` (`BURST · 200 instant payments · 4 at once`) and whose right end carries its clock in `foreground-meta` — the same captioned-hairline shape the still-open strip uses, so a region that has taken the screen still reads as part of the same surface. That rule's right end also carries `rule-feed-status` — the **Feed status, inline** component in its own `meta` tokens — exactly as the still-open strip's rule carries it. The takeover replaces every surface that says whether the console is listening, so it inherits that statement rather than deleting it: `still moving` is a count of payments the console is currently listening for and is unreadable without it, and a takeover able to show a figure nobody is waiting on would be the one failure this whole surface exists to prevent. The run clock shares that end and yields to it: where the line cannot hold both, the clock is what goes — a duration the counters already imply — and the feed statement is surrendered at no width, exactly as the fault slider's value column is never sacrificed to preserve its track. Its one control sits alone at the takeover's lower right in the object-anchored treatment (`dismiss-action-border`/`-foreground`/`-fill`), and the slot never holds two meanings at once: both of its labels land in the same cells, exactly as a **Destructive action button**'s resolved action reoccupies its own slot. When the takeover appears, how long it holds, and which label the slot carries are behaviour — see `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns, *Burst takeover*.
- **Outcome query** (`{components.outcome-query}`) — no longer a standing captioned field with a label of its own; it is the focus card's always-available action, rendered in the same `action-slot-width` and the same object-anchored treatment as Cancel (single `border`, `fill: none`, `foreground` `text-primary`), printed `[ Look up outcome ]`. **This supersedes the "outcome key" field** of earlier revisions: a permanently visible input, for an id the operator would have to type by hand, spent standing rows to offer what one action slot offers on the payment already in front of the room. `always-enabled: true` is unchanged and is the whole point of the component: unlike every other mutating control it never dims or disables during the single-action-in-flight lock, rendering identically whether or not another action is in flight — a visual promise, not just a behavioral one, that read-only lookup is always available. It still takes no teal outline for that: never dimming is not the same as being lock-exempt, and the signature belongs to controls that may *mutate* while the lock is held.
- **Transient announcement** (`{components.transient-announcement}`) — a single row (`height: 1row`) at the foot of the content area, `surface-base`, borderless, where the console says something now that the evidence line is gone, on the row immediately above the inline feed status. **The row takes the token of the thing it announces, never a fixed one.** A refused or failed command renders `symbol` (`✕`, `fallback-symbol` `[X]`) and its sentence in `foreground` (`state-failed`) — a command that ran and was refused is a proven failure, which is exactly what that token is reserved for. A notice that is not a verdict — a lost feed, a reconnection, a palette switch — renders `symbol-notice` (`○`, `fallback-symbol-notice` `[ ]`) and its sentence in `foreground-notice` (`state-neutral`), the same tone the rows behind it are taking at that instant, because the largest single-line statement on the screen must never assert a failure that the states it describes do not. **This supersedes this component's earlier single-valued spec**, which gave every announcement the proven-failure token and would have announced a dropped subscription in red above rows correctly turning gray. Neither form adds a colour: both reuse tokens both palettes already hold. It takes no overlay tone, no border and no fill, because it is a line of the content area rather than a layer above it, and it occupies no reserved band: nothing is held empty against its arrival, and the rows return to the region when it clears. **It is an announcement, never a record.** Everything it can say is written durably in the Evidence workspace first (see Do's and Don'ts; why that is what makes the removed band safe to remove, and how long the row stays, are argued once in `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture, *Override — the bottom band is removed from every workspace*, and Component Patterns).
- **Lock-exempt control signature** — a cross-cutting rule rather than a component: every control that stays live while the global single-action-in-flight lock is held and sits among controls that do not renders as a single `accent-teal` outline with **no fill**, and never dims. The exempt membership is the burst control's Cancel, every control in the Faults workspace, and panic-off — enumerated once in `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns, *Single action in flight*. **This supersedes the earlier count of "exactly three controls"**, which counted the signature's wearers and read as the membership. The wearers are the burst Cancel, every **Fault slider**, and the **Panic-off control**: inside Faults, where the whole workspace stays live, exemption is a property of the workspace rather than of each control, which is why the **Apply faults action** keeps its `primary-action-button` fill and the **Fault preset chips** keep the shared chip language without either being an omission. The signature exists so that when the console dims for an in-flight action, the handful of controls that remain usable read as one recognizable family at a glance, instead of each looking like an unexplained local inconsistency. No control may adopt this treatment without being genuinely lock-exempt, and no lock-exempt control that stands among locked ones may omit it.
- **Fault arming toggle** (`{components.fault-arming-toggle}`) — a two-state pill in the Resources workspace, replacing the former DevProxy on/off toggle: `armed-background`/`armed-foreground` (filled `accent-teal`/`surface-base`) when the next AppHost start will bring up a Dev Proxy, `unarmed-background`/`unarmed-foreground` (`surface-raised`/`text-secondary`) when it will not, and `unavailable-foreground` (`text-muted`, no fill) on an Attached topology this session cannot re-arm. Its caption always names the launch-time truth ("faults armed on next start"), never a bare "on" — the visual weight of a filled teal pill would otherwise read as "faults are happening now," which it does not mean.
- **Fault slider** (`{components.fault-slider}`) — one horizontal row per knob on `surface-base`, borderless: a `label`-typography caption on the left, a `track-width` bar, and a fixed `value-column` to the right of the track that always renders the exact numeric value in `body` typography. The track is drawn in `track` (`border-hairline`); the portion representing the **currently live** level fills in `fill-live` (`accent-teal`, the lock-exempt signature), and any **staged** level not yet applied fills in `fill-staged` (`text-secondary`) with both numbers shown in the value column as an explicit delta (`5% → 40%`). The `handle` is `text-primary`, reverse-video on focus. The latency knob carries two handles (floor and ceiling) on one track. Disabled (unarmed/Attached topology) renders label, track, and value in `disabled-foreground` — the value stays legible, because the operator must still be able to read what *would* be applied. **No token encodes severity**: the bar's fill length and the printed number are the only severity channels (see Colors).
- **Fault preset chips** (`{components.fault-preset-chip}`) — a segmented chip row using the same `chip-selected-*`/`chip-unselected-*` language as the idempotency selector and the compose bar's rail chips, so "pick one of a small fixed set" looks identical everywhere in the console. When any knob is moved off its preset, the row's selection clears and a `custom-label-foreground` (`text-secondary`) caption reads `Custom` — the console never leaves a preset chip looking selected while the values no longer match it.
- **Apply faults action** (`{components.apply-faults-button}`) — the Faults workspace's one `primary-action-button`, anchored in the same fixed lower action region as the primary action of Resources and Load Test. It is **not** given `destructive-action-button` tokens: faults destroy no state, are fully reversible, and are undone by one key, and dressing them in the double-bordered red treatment would blunt that treatment where it actually guards data loss. With nothing staged it renders `disabled-foreground`/`disabled-fill: none` in the same slot; while staged, `staged-delta-foreground` (`text-secondary`) prints the count of knobs about to change in its caption.
- **Panic-off control** (`{components.panic-off-control}`) — single `accent-teal` outline, no fill, `always-enabled`, carrying the lock-exempt signature it shares with burst Cancel and the sliders. Bound to `0` from every workspace. It never renders as a `destructive-action-button` and never opens the confirmation modal: it is the console's one control whose entire purpose is to make the running system safer, and gating it would invert the meaning of every other gate in this file.
- **Replica indicator** (`{components.replica-indicator}`) — a bare `text-secondary` numeral, for example "×3"; the `stepper-border`/`stepper-foreground` (single border, `accent-teal`) increment/decrement control renders only when the current Aspire command surface actually supports scaling that resource — otherwise the numeral appears alone, with no button token at all, never a disabled-looking button that implies scaling should work.
- **Load phase strip** (`{components.load-phase-strip}`) — five fixed cells in a single horizontal row, divided by single-hairline verticals, in the fixed sequence `Reset → Run → Wait → Assert → Investigate` (Reset is the workflow's first internal phase, never a separate/standalone control — see `EXPERIENCE.md` Component Patterns, Load phase strip). Each cell shows `cell-not-yet-reached` (`state-neutral`) before it starts, `cell-active` (`state-running`, `~`, plus an elapsed-time readout) while running, and resolves to `cell-passed` (`state-healthy`) or `cell-failed` (`state-failed`). Before any run this session, all five cells show `cell-not-yet-reached`.
- **Invariant chip** (`{components.invariant-chip}`) — one chip per invariant (exactly-once, no-loss, balance-conserved, ordering, drained) plus a distinct inline-instant-settlement chip: single-`border` (unlike the borderless resource status chips, so the two families are never visually confused), `foreground-pass`/`foreground-fail`/`foreground-not-yet-observed` (`state-healthy`/`state-failed`/`state-neutral`) each shown individually, never collapsed into one pass/fail bit. Before any run this session, all chips show `foreground-not-yet-observed`.
- **Primary action button** (`{components.primary-action-button}`) — `accent-teal` fill, single border, reverse-video on focus. Exactly one exists per screen at a time. In Resources, Faults and Load Test it is anchored in that workspace's fixed lower action region (see `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture), which now ends the content area itself rather than sitting above an evidence strip, so the operator's hand and eye never have to relocate it mid-sentence. **Operations overrides that placement, and only Operations**: its Submit is anchored at the right edge of the **Compose bar**'s first line, and the payment's own action at the right edge of the **Focus card** — in a workspace built around one selected payment, an action reads as belonging to the object it operates on, and a Cancel parked at the bottom of the screen would be a control for *some* payment rather than for the one being watched. The rule it replaces — one fixed lower slot per workspace — still governs everywhere else, and the override buys nothing outside this workspace. When a workspace's primary action is itself destructive (the Load Test workspace's **Run**, which resets the disposable topology as its first phase — see Load phase strip), it instead renders with `destructive-action-button` tokens, not `primary-action-button` tokens, and is gated by the same **Confirmation modal**.
- **Destructive action button** (`{components.destructive-action-button}`) — `surface-raised` fill with `state-failed-on-raised` text (a lightened "on-raised" tint, not the base `state-failed` hex, chosen because `state-failed` itself measures a sub-AA 4.12:1 on `surface-raised` while `state-failed-on-raised` measures 4.91:1 — see Colors; never a solid red fill either way, so a stray glance must not read "already failed"), double border, gated by the **Confirmation modal**'s single typed confirmation key (`Y`) — no hold gesture, no double-Enter. Its resolved action, such as "Restart," always renders in the exact screen position the destructive action ("Stop") occupied. Applies to resource Stop/Restart, AppHost Stop/Switch, and Load Test Run.
- **Evidence strip — removed** (component and its token group deleted from this file). Earlier revisions gave the shell a persistent one-row strip along the bottom which, with the primary action button above it, formed a fixed lower action/**detail** region; Operations added an in-flight action line above that, and a footer shortcut strip ran beneath — three rows of chrome in every workspace, present whether or not they had anything to say. All three are gone, in all five workspaces: `{spacing.shell-chrome-height}` is now 7 rows of 30 rather than 10 (see **Layout & Spacing**), and this file's own token group for the strip is deleted with it. What the removed rows used to say is said by surfaces that already exist — the **Transient announcement** for a refused or failed command, the **Focus card** for an arriving outcome, and the Evidence workspace for the durable record beneath both (the case for the removal, and why nothing truthful is lost with it, is argued once in `EXPERIENCE.md` Information Architecture, *Override — the bottom band is removed from every workspace*). Resources, Faults and Load Test keep their fixed lower **action** region; what every workspace loses is the detail band beneath it.
- **Confirmation modal** (`{components.confirmation-modal}`) — `surface-overlay`, double border, appears only for a disruptive resource/topology/Load-Test-Run command, always names the exact target and exact command before it can be confirmed. Opens with focus on **Cancel** (`focus-on-open: cancel`, the safer default), traps focus entirely — nothing else on screen is reachable while it is open — and requires the single explicit confirmation key `Y` (`confirm-key`); `Escape` (`cancel-key`) cancels. On either confirm or cancel, focus returns to the exact control that opened it. No hold-to-confirm gesture and no double-Enter confirmation exist anywhere in this console.

## Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Pair every status chip with a symbol and a text label | Communicate state through color alone |
| Reserve `state-failed` (red) for a proven failure | Use red for "in progress," "unknown," or "ambiguous" |
| Reserve `state-healthy` (green) for a proven pass/healthy resource | Use green optimistically before evidence exists |
| Keep the primary action in one fixed screen position per workspace (Operations excepted — see the object-anchoring row below) | Relocate the primary action based on content length |
| Render raw evidence (`evidence-mono`) unstyled and byte-faithful, pannable, wrap-toggle optional | Reformat, summarize, or truncate a raw response body silently |
| Keep the confirmation modal one layer deep, double-bordered, opened with Cancel focused | Stack a second modal, or open a confirmation with the destructive action pre-focused |
| Gate every destructive action with the single typed confirmation key (`Y`) | Use a hold-to-confirm gesture or accept double-Enter as confirmation |
| Let the terminal emulator's font size own legibility | Simulate "small print" or a text-size-based meaning distinction |
| Keep the work surface on one continuous `surface-base` tone with hairline dividers | Box workspace content in a raised "panel" background |
| Keep workspace navigation in the compact, persistent left rail with one-key shortcuts | Reintroduce a horizontal tab strip or nest navigation |
| Show the fault chip persistently in the topology bar across every workspace | Let fault injection stay silently in force after leaving the Faults workspace |
| Carry severity in the printed number, the bar fill length, and the label | Encode fault severity in color, or add a green-amber-red severity ramp |
| Reserve `accent-teal` for its three stated roles — primary action, focus ring, lock-exempt/faults-in-force | Use `accent-teal` decoratively or as a second competing accent, let a fourth teal role appear, or give a pass/fail state the teal treatment |
| Give every lock-exempt control that stands among locked ones the same outline-teal-no-fill signature | Style a control as lock-exempt when it is not, or dim one that is |
| Render a staged level and its live level together as an explicit delta | Show a staged number alone, where it can be misread as the current level |
| Label the arming toggle with its launch-time meaning | Let a filled teal arming pill imply faults are being injected right now |
| Keep Apply on `primary-action-button` tokens and panic-off always enabled | Dress a reversible fault change in the destructive red/double-border treatment |
| Re-state an unresolved payment as `Outcome unknown` (`state-neutral`) when the feed drops | Render a dropped subscription in red, asserting a payment failure nothing proved |
| Reuse `state-healthy`/`state-failed`/`state-running` for settled/rejected/awaiting | Add a settlement-specific color token, or give feed status the teal treatment |
| Carry the active workspace on the `▸` marker, with any `accent-navy` fill as reinforcement | Let the fill alone say which workspace is active |
| Give both palettes their own measured hexes against their own canvas | Reuse the dark hexes on a light background, where eight of ten fall below AA |
| Keep light and dark differing in token values only | Let a component, state, symbol, or behaviour vary by palette |
| Distinguish a system-produced row by its inbound gutter marker | Rely on color or position alone to separate what the bank said from what the operator did |
| Carry the focus card's state in the word itself — bold, upper case, fixed column, symbol beside it | Let colour be what tells the room what happened to a payment |
| Render a withdrawn payment in `state-neutral`, the palette's "nothing was proven wrong" tone | Dress a cancellation in `state-failed` or the destructive red/double-border treatment |
| Keep the transient announcement a second copy of something the Evidence workspace already holds | Let a line that will clear be the only place an outcome was ever shown |
| Print the burst's Sent and Settled figures in full and let the bar reinforce them | Let the proportional bar stand in for the counts, or colour it as a verdict |
| Let every workspace end at the bottom of its own content area | Reintroduce a persistent bottom band — footer strip, in-flight line, or evidence line |
| Anchor a payment's action to the payment (Operations only), and say where the rule is overridden | Let a second filled-teal primary action appear on one screen |
| Let the still-open strip vanish whole — rule included — whenever fewer than two payments are open | Hold an empty list, a placeholder row, or a one-row strip restating the payment the card is already showing |
| Print the burst's **Sent** figures in `text-primary` — that line reports transport, not proof | Colour an `accepted` or a `failed` send as a proven pass or a proven failure |
| Give a transient announcement the token of the thing it announces — red for a refusal, `state-neutral` for a notice | Announce a lost feed in the proven-failure red that the rows behind it are correctly not taking |
| Carry the feed statement onto whatever surface has taken the screen, the burst takeover included | Let a surface show a count of payments the console is waiting on without saying whether it is listening |
| Stamp the resting focus card with its own payment's wall-clock submit time | Let a payment resolved twenty minutes and one topology ago read as one that just happened |
